five-point

It also covers who can open the data from outside the EU, because the GDPR treats that access as a transfer.

Key takeaways
Because an EU region fixes only where data is stored and computed, ask every provider about support access, subprocessors and non-EU law too.
Support staff who open your data from outside the EU create a GDPR transfer, so get each provider's support locations in writing.

What EU data residency means for AI and analytics workloads
EU data residency applies to every tool in an AI or analytics pipeline that touches the data. A customer contract or a data protection officer usually asks for it in one line. So which parts of an AI or analytics stack does that line cover, and how do you check a provider?
Take one example, which is hypothetical. A Berlin SaaS team summarises support tickets with a large language model (LLM) and charts the results in an analytics dashboard. One ticket makes four stops:
At rest:
it is stored in the team's data warehouse.
In processing:
it is sent to an LLM API, which reads it and writes a summary.
Support access:
an engineer at one of the vendors opens it to debug a failed job.
Subprocessors:
each tool hands parts of the work to its own vendors, such as hosting or payment providers.
Residency has to hold at every stop. A warehouse in Frankfurt does not help if the LLM call runs in Virginia. The example is simplified: it does not show how likely each gap is, or what closing it costs. We built Lyceum as a sovereign European AI inference provider for open-source models, and our
serverless models run in Paris and Finland
. The last section shows how we answer each stop, and
AI data residency, explained properly
covers the storage and processing split in depth.
An EU region answers where your data sits; who controls the stack and which laws reach it is settled by the provider's entity, access rules, subprocessors and contract.

Why an EU region alone does not settle residency
An EU region settles where the bytes sit. It does not settle who can reach them, and the GDPR cares about both.
The GDPR has no data-localisation rule. Article 44 allows transfers outside the EEA only under Chapter V, the GDPR's rules for international transfers. The European Data Protection Board (EDPB), the body of EU data protection authorities, adds the access point. Its Guidelines 05/2021 treat remote access from a third country as a transfer. That holds "even if it takes place only by means of displaying personal data on a screen", for example in support situations.
The same guidelines name a second case: an EU processor that is subject to third-country law. Such a processor may receive government access requests. GDPR Article 28(1) lets you use only processors that give sufficient guarantees, and for this processor, the EDPB writes, reliability "may be in doubt".
The US CLOUD Act is the usual example. The law dates from March 2018. It obliges US cloud and communication providers to disclose data in their "possession, custody, or control", wherever it is located. The US Department of Justice describes it as a tool to reach evidence held by US-based global providers. GDPR Article 48 answers from the EU side. A third-country order is recognised only when it rests on an international agreement, such as a legal assistance treaty. Without one, the order alone gives the provider no GDPR basis to hand the data over.
Article 83(5) sets the stakes. Breaking the transfer rules can cost up to €20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. We explain the hyperscaler case in

, and the wider argument in

EU-US Data Privacy Framework status in October 2026
The EU-US Data Privacy Framework (DPF) is still in force in October 2026. It is an adequacy decision, adopted on 10 July 2023. That is the Commission's finding that participating US companies protect personal data well enough for it to flow to them freely. The Commission's adequacy list still names the United States for those companies, next to Switzerland, the United Kingdom and Japan.
The decision is under challenge. The General Court dismissed Philippe Latombe's action on 3 September 2025. He appealed to the Court of Justice on 31 October 2025, asking it to annul the decision (Case C-703/25 P). Until that court rules, the DPF applies. Its safeguards limit US intelligence access to what is necessary and proportionate, answering the Court of Justice's Schrems II judgment of July 2020.
We recommend the example team does two things. First, check that each US analytics or LLM vendor appears on the Data Privacy Framework list. Second, make the DPA name a fallback in case the court annuls the decision. That fallback is usually standard contractual clauses (SCCs), the Commission's model transfer contract.

Summary
EU data residency holds only when five checks pass for every tool that touches your data: where it is stored, where it is processed, who can open it, which subprocessors receive it and which non-EU laws reach the provider. An EU region settles the first two, while the other three depend on what each provider commits to in writing. For LLM calls on our API, our DPA and legal page make that commitment for all five.
If a customer contract requires EU processing for your LLM calls, send our DPA to your reviewer first. You can read it on
our legal page
, and request the subprocessor list and our TOMs (technical and organisational measures) there.

Frequently asked questions
Does the GDPR require data to stay in the EU?
No. Transfers outside the EEA are lawful with an adequacy decision, with safeguards such as standard contractual clauses, or under narrow exceptions such as explicit consent.
Is Switzerland part of EU data residency?
Switzerland is outside the EU and the EEA. The European Commission recognises it as adequate, so personal data can flow there without extra transfer safeguards.
Does EU data residency apply to analytics tools?
Yes. Event data, warehouse tables and dashboards hold personal data, so the same five checks apply. A US analytics vendor also needs a Data Privacy Framework listing or standard contractual clauses.
Where do Lyceum Serverless Inference models run?
In Paris and Finland, on GPUs in European data centres. Prompts and outputs are processed, not stored, and never used for training.
