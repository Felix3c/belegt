Challenge

Why is AI compliance so painful?

Using AI models directly means every provider becomes your problem: another sub-processor, another retention policy, another region, another review.

Every model is a new sub-processor

Each provider you integrate directly must be added to your sub-processor list, with a DPA amendment, customer notification, and a 30-day objection window before you can ship.

Unknown retention policies

Every provider retains prompts differently, some keep content for abuse monitoring, some publish no retention period at all, and your DPO has to chase each one.

Data leaves the EU by default

Most default endpoints process requests in the US, which makes transfer assessments, SCCs, and Schrems II analysis your problem for every single provider.

A security review per vendor

Five providers means five vendor assessments, five sets of terms, and five renewals, and the list grows every time a better model ships.

FAQ

AI security & compliance FAQ

Is it GDPR-compliant to use OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google models?

+
It can be, and what matters is where the data is processed, what the provider retains, and whether your DPA covers the flow. Opper gives you EU-hosted routes for major models, acts as your single sub-processor, and provides a standard DPA with SCCs, so you can use frontier models while keeping processing in the EU and your paperwork unchanged.

What is zero data retention (ZDR)?

+
Zero data retention means the model provider does not store your prompts or completions after the request is served, and runs no abuse monitoring that could hold them. A large share of the EU routes carry it on all traffic by default, which is rarer across the category than the marketing suggests, and you can filter for it live in the model directory, either every model with zero data retention or only EU-hosted routes with zero data retention. Routes where content is kept only for abuse monitoring are marked Abuse monitoring, with their window; routes that keep content for other purposes are marked Retained. For OpenAI models on Azure and Gemini on Google Vertex, zero data retention without abuse monitoring is available under a signed Enterprise agreement; Claude on AWS Bedrock is zero data retention on pay-as-you-go.

Do I need a DPA with every model provider?

+
Not when you route through Opper. You sign one DPA with Opper, and Opper maintains the sub-processor relationships with the model providers, so adding a new model does not require amending your DPA, notifying your customers, or waiting out a 30-day objection window. Opper's own sub-processors, every model provider included, are published on the trust center.

Where is my data processed?

+
The Opper platform runs in AWS Stockholm, Sweden, and inference requests are processed in the region of the route you select. EU routes keep data in the EU end to end on every plan, pay-as-you-go included: call an EU route's model id, or set a Model access rule that allows only EU inference locations for the whole organization. A model name without a provider prefix can be served from any region in its pool, and every EU route is listed at opper.ai/models/eu. Opper can also be deployed onto other clouds and regions, European sovereign providers or elsewhere, as it already runs on evroc for high-privacy workloads.

Does Opper store my prompts?

+
Not by default. Opper stores metadata like model, token counts, latency, and cost for your analytics, while prompt and completion content is not retained. On top of that, many EU routes carry provider-side zero data retention by default, so prompts are not kept at either hop, and the model directory filters on it. With Control Plane, a retention rule turns tracing on for the period you choose, and a zero data retention policy in Rules restricts calls to providers that keep nothing.

Does Opper train on my data?

+
No, Opper never trains on your data. Each model provider's training posture is published on the trust center and in the provider directory, and a Zero data retention rule in Model access, on Control Plane, keeps every call away from providers that train on or log your content.

How does Opper support EU AI Act, DORA, or NIS2 reviews?

+
Every call is logged with model, cost, latency, and policy decisions, and the Control Plane adds full traces, retention rules, spend limits, and model allowlists. That gives compliance teams the audit trail and documentation these reviews ask for.

Is Opper ISO 27001 certified?

+
Yes. Opper Technology AB is certified against ISO/IEC 27001:2022, the international standard for information security management. The controls it covers and the policies behind them are published on the Opper trust center at trust.opper.ai, next to the GDPR posture and the sub-processor register, the certificate itself is available there on request, and the trust center is continuously monitored rather than refreshed once a year.

What security and compliance documents can I get from Opper?

+
The standard Data Processing Agreement and the platform security overview are published on opper.ai, the sub-processor register is on the trust center, Standard Contractual Clauses are available on request, we sign the standard DPA on request, on Control Plane a model access rule limits the model providers that process your data to the ones you allow, and Enterprise agreements can carry tailored terms and extend zero retention to the usage metadata too. The ISO/IEC 27001:2022 controls and policies are published on the Opper trust center at trust.opper.ai, where the certificate is available on request.
